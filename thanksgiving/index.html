Every year, New York’s worst day for kitchen fires is Thanksgiving.

Twelve years of fire department calls, one day at a time.

Each row is a year. Each sliver is one day, shaded by how many times the FDNY was called to a stove fire in a New York City apartment building. DarkerBrighter means more.

Look closer at November and December. Christmas Eve, Christmas and New Year’s Eve make straight columns, because they fall on the same date every year.

The darkestbrightest mark in each row does not. It moves around between Nov. 22 and Nov. 28.

Slide each year so its fourth Thursday of November lines up, and the darkbright marks stack into one column.

Thanksgiving was the city’s worst day for stove fires in all 12 years from 2014 to 2025. Older records show the same thing every year back to 2005.

Thanksgiving brings four to five times as many calls as a typical day of the same year. The record was 126 in 2018.

The fires follow the turkey. On Thanksgiving they climb all morning and peak at 2 p.m., about eight times a normal November Thursday at that hour.

By 8 p.m., with dinner on the table, they are down by three quarters.

Thanksgiving 2026 is Nov. 26.

If the last five years are any guide, expect about a hundred calls.

Source: FDNY Fire Incident Dispatch Data, NYC Open Data. Counts are dispatches classified “Multiple Dwelling ‘A’: food on the stove fire,” the fire department’s category for stove fires in apartment buildings; houses are filed separately and are not included. A typical day is the median day of that year. The chart starts in 2014: earlier years show about 50% more calls on every day, likely a change in how calls were coded, and Thanksgiving was the worst day in each of those years as well.
